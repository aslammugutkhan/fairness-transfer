# Model card: BN-LFA (local factor adaptation for equalized-odds transfer)

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Model | Discrete Bayesian network over R (race), X (sex), C (age), E (education), W (class of worker), O (occupation), H (hours), Y (income > \$50k). Composition CPTs with required R, X parents and learned remaining parents (hill climbing on validation log-likelihood, tiers, in-degree ≤ 4), hierarchical Dirichlet smoothing; log-linear outcome CPD (main effects + sex interactions, L2, C = 1), tabulated. Learned structure (identical in all six domains): P(R) P(X∣R) P(C∣R,X) P(E∣R,X,C) P(O∣R,X,C,E) P(W∣R,X,C,O) P(H∣R,X,C,O) P(Y∣R,X,C,E,W,O,H); 7,919 composition parameters and 75 outcome coefficients |
| Adaptation | From n labelled target persons: Bayes-factor evidence of shift per factor, exact decision impact, at most two factors updated, equalized-odds thresholds re-selected on the source validation split reweighted by the updated factors' density ratio (`docs/proposal.md`; notebook section 1.8) |
| Intended use | Research benchmark: studying fairness transfer across ACS states and years under a fixed target-label budget, and auditing which factors shifted |
| Out of scope | Any decision about real people (credit, hiring, benefits, pay, policing); inferring anyone's income or protected attributes; claims about states, years or populations not studied; causal claims about why incomes differ |
| Decision | D = 1: predicted ACSIncome label (income > \$50,000). A favourable benchmark classification, not merit or need |
| Inputs at prediction time | Age, education, class of worker, occupation group, hours, **and recorded race and sex**. Race and sex enter the score model as features and select the group-specific threshold. Every method in this project except LR-S, GBT-S and RW-S uses group thresholds; all score models use R and X as features |
| Training data | ACS PUMS 1-Year 2018 person files of CA, TX, NY (household-disjoint train/validation splits); adaptation samples from the target's household-disjoint pool |
| Evaluation data | Household-disjoint 20 % test splits of CA, TX, NY 2018 and 2019 (20,681–39,551 persons); unweighted benchmark sample (survey-weighted secondary) |
| Within-domain performance (mean of six domains) | LR-S AUROC 0.869, balanced accuracy 0.785, ΔEOdds 0.423 (no mitigation). Source fair rules: EO-S ΔEOdds 0.124 at BA 0.771; BN-S (model-based thresholds) 0.140 at 0.769. Log loss 0.441, Brier 0.144 |
| Transfer performance (mean of nine transfers, n = 1,000) | BN-LFA ΔEOdds 0.140 (seed sd 0.005), BA 0.770, AUROC 0.867, log loss 0.445, Brier 0.146; worst transfer ΔEOdds 0.171 (NY18 → TX18). Comparators: EO-S 0.150, TT 0.205, IW-EO 0.156, BN-all 0.147 at BA 0.769–0.772 |
| Group performance (BN-LFA, n = 1,000, groups with ≥ 100 positives and negatives) | TPR 0.64–0.89 and FPR 0.12–0.37 across the 76 (transfer, group) cells; lowest TPRs for Black women (0.64 in one transfer), Other-race men (0.66) and Two-or-more-races women (0.67); highest FPRs for Other-race men (up to 0.37). Full table with denominators: `results/tables/per_group_summary_n1000.csv` |
| Small groups | American Indian, Alaska Native, AIAN-other and NHPI cells (52 of 147 transfer × group cells at n = 1,000 have fewer than 30 positives or negatives) are reported with denominators and flagged exploratory; their error rates are not estimable reliably and no fairness claim is made for them |
| Calibration | Score model LR-S (identical to the BN outcome CPD, so BN-S has the same scores). Groups with primary support: within domain, ECE 0.030 on average (max 0.066), mean score − positive rate within ±0.037; after zero-label transfer ECE 0.046 on average (max 0.102, Asian women and Other-race men in TX), mean score − positive rate from −0.078 to +0.101 (`results/metrics/group_calibration.csv`) |
| Probabilistic quality of the adapted network | Held-out joint log-likelihood per person on the target test split: source BN −8.898; BN-LFA −8.873 (n = 200) and −8.878 (n = 1,000); BN-all −8.926 (n = 200, worse than not adapting) and −8.821 (n = 1,000) (`results/tables/adapted_bn_heldout_loglik_summary.csv`) |
| Known limitations | Associational graph; tier ordering is an assumption; approximate conditional independences bias the model-based audit (89 % of group TPRs within sampling noise); no race interactions in the outcome CPD; the fixed nominal \$50k threshold mixes wage growth into the 2018 → 2019 shift; deterministic thresholds cannot equalize exactly; three states and two years only |
| Fairness caveats | Equalized odds is measured against the recorded income label, which reflects historical labour-market inequality; equalizing error rates does not make the label fair. Group-specific thresholds treat people differently by recorded race and sex, which may be unlawful in real decision settings. The primary ≥ 100 support rule excludes the smallest groups from the headline gap |
| Evidence | `results/metrics/confirmatory_tests_holm.csv`, `results/tables/exp_transfer_summary.csv`, report Section 4 |
